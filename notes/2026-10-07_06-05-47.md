They do different jobs: observation time measures the speed, and the output timeframe sets how long the generated pattern is.

- **Observation time (seconds)** is how long you watched the video while counting cycles. You enter it together with **Observed full cycles**, and the two numbers give the tempo you measured. Nothing is generated over this span.
  - Example: 12 cycles in 30 s gives 24 cycles/min, so one cycle lasts 2.5 s.

- **Output timeframe (seconds)** is how much of the timeline the inserted pattern fills, starting at the playhead.
  - The tool suggests a whole number of cycles from the measured tempo: `round(observedCycles × outputSeconds / observedSeconds)`, minimum 1.
  - It then stretches or squeezes those cycles slightly so they end exactly at the end of the timeframe, with no half cycle at the end.

Example (test case G05 in `VERIFICATION.md:88`): you observe 12 cycles in 30 s and set a 12 s output.
- The suggested count is 5 cycles, so the effective tempo is 25 cycles/min against the 24 you measured.
- The dialog shows both numbers so you can see the adjustment.
- If you override the count to 4, the tempo becomes 20 cycles/min.

This lets you measure over a long stretch for accuracy, like several minutes, and still insert a short section or a longer one. The relevant code is in `PatternGenerator.cs:39-60` and `PatternInsertionDialog.cs:53-59`.
