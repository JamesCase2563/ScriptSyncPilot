Yes—this could make the editor much easier to think in. The workflow in `scripting-workflow.md` is built around placing and then converting lots of timeline points. An **action-first editor** could let people describe the movement they want, and treat the funscript points as generated output.

For example, an action list might read:

1. Move to **20%** over **300 ms**
2. Hold for **1.2 seconds**
3. Alternate between **20% and 80%**, **10 cycles over 15 seconds**
4. Hold at **20%** for **2 seconds**

I’d keep the actions as the editable source, with a preview that shows the resulting movement over the video. Saving the project would preserve those actions; exporting would compile them into a funscript. That way, changing “10 cycles” to “12” wouldn’t mean editing a large set of generated points by hand.

A few details would matter for making this feel predictable:

- Make clear whether durations include the move itself or only the hold afterward.
- Define what happens at an action’s end—does the next action continue from the current position, jump, or transition smoothly?
- Show the generated motion on the timeline, so users can still inspect and fine-tune individual points when needed.
- Let users set an action’s start time and duration, and preview it in context.
- Preserve hand-edited points, or make it clear when regenerating an action will replace them.

I’d start with a small set of composable actions—**Move**, **Hold**, and **Repeat/Alternate**—rather than trying to model every possible movement pattern up front. It seems like a promising way to support both quick, high-level scripting and precise cleanup.
