**For the CRA, the remaining facts to establish are:**

- Is source genuinely the only distribution, or are binaries, installers, or packages also published?
- Is there any commercial activity associated with development, distribution, or support—such as paid support, sponsorship, advertising, or other income or benefit?
- Who is the publisher legally: an individual or an organization, and where is it established?
- Does the project provide any service itself, or does the app connect only to Handy’s services? Does any organization involved meet the CRA’s definition of an open-source software steward?

Free MIT licensing alone does not answer CRA applicability. Those facts need to be assessed against the [CRA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj) by someone qualified to advise on it.

**For GDPR and other obligations**, the key question is whether the project maintainer itself receives or controls personal data—not merely whether the app sends data to Handy. Confirm whether there is telemetry, crash reporting, or collection through support. Separately document what the app sends to Handy, what remains on the user’s machine, and which party is responsible for that processing.

Other sensible legal-review areas are third-party copyright/license notices, security-report handling, and product liability or safety expectations for software that controls a physical device. Disclaimers can communicate limits, but they don’t replace applicable duties.
