For the **Legal scope** item, you’ve provided the main project facts. I don’t think you need to make more product-policy decisions right now; what remains is **verification and legal assessment**:

- Verify the stated model in practice: public source only, no binaries or service, no commercial activity, and no telemetry or support-data collection by the maintainer.
- Document exactly what the app sends to Handy and what Handy receives. Even if the maintainer never sees it, the connection key and uploaded script need to be considered in the privacy review; whether they are personal data depends on context and linkability.
- Have qualified counsel assess the CRA and Norwegian/EEA obligations against those verified facts, including whether any open-source steward role applies. For GDPR, distinguish the maintainer’s own data handling from the app’s direct communication with Handy.
- Keep “other obligations” focused on concrete concerns: third-party license notices, security-report handling, and any relevant product-safety or liability expectations.

So this could be reframed as **“Legal applicability assessment”** rather than an open product decision. The CRA and GDPR conclusions should remain open until the facts are checked and appropriately reviewed.
