# Splitting into DECISIONS.md and VERIFICATION.md

## Why it works

The proposal currently mixes three lifecycles in one file:

1. **The plan itself** (gates, open risks) — changes until release, then freezes.
2. **Decisions already recorded** — append-only history; should never be rewritten, only added to.
3. **Evidence + go/no-go** — doesn't exist yet; grows during verification.

Splitting along those lines means each document has one job and one change pattern. It also directly fixes the biggest thing the proposal lost from the current plan: the **dated decision trail**. A dedicated decision log is the natural home for "scope approved 2026-10-24 by maintainer," "paid legal review declined 2026-10-24," etc.

## Suggested structure

| File | Contains | Lifecycle |
|---|---|---|
| `PLAN.md` (adopted proposal) | Goal, release gates, open risks, ownership | Edited until release, then archived |
| `DECISIONS.md` | Dated, append-only log: decision, who, date, rationale, link to evidence (ADR-lite, a table or short entries is enough) | Append-only, survives the plan |
| `VERIFICATION.md` | Per-gate evidence links, unresolved items, final go/no-go record | Created when verification starts — the proposal already designates this |

The proposal's "Decisions and evidence already recorded" section becomes the seed content of `DECISIONS.md`, re-expanded with the dates and attributions from the current `PLAN.md`. The plan then just links: *"Decisions are recorded in DECISIONS.md; gate evidence in VERIFICATION.md."*

## Two cautions

- **Don't over-split.** You already have `SCOPE-AND-LIMITATIONS.md` and `TESTED-ENVIRONMENTS.md` in this folder. The proposal's "Product baseline" section largely restates those — with the split, I'd shrink it to two sentences plus links rather than creating yet another baseline document. For a solo-maintainer project, four focused files in one folder is the sweet spot; more becomes maintenance drag.
- **No duplication rule.** Each fact lives in exactly one file; everything else links. Otherwise the CRA caveats and "not legal advice" qualifiers will drift apart across copies — the one failure mode that matters for this particular content.

One bonus: with decisions externalized, the eventual archival story is clean — when release happens, `PLAN.md` moves to `dev/archive/` like the credential-protection work did, while `DECISIONS.md` stays live for future reassessments (e.g., the "reassess CRA if monetisation changes" trigger).
